# Argus Philosophy

## Evidence before conclusions

Argus exists to make information analysis inspectable. A useful output must
show its sources, observations, method, assumptions, limitations, and the path
from evidence to conclusion.

## Documents describe reality; they are not reality

A document proves that particular content was published in a particular
context. It does not prove that every statement inside it is true. Several
documents may describe one event, and one document may describe several
events. Argus therefore keeps information-layer observations separate from
reconstructed events and analytical hypotheses.

## Measurement before interpretation

Counts, spans, timestamps, identities, similarities, and disagreements should
be stored before broader interpretations are attempted. A score must be
decomposable into its components and must not be labelled a probability unless
it has been calibrated for the conditions in which it is used.

## Uncertainty is data

Missing inputs, disputed identities, contradictory evidence, unavailable
sources, and method limitations remain visible. Argus should fail closed at
unsafe analytical boundaries rather than silently substituting a guess.

## Reproducibility is part of the result

Inputs are immutable or content-addressed. Methods, schemas, software
identities, attempts, and evidence locations are versioned. Re-running an
analysis on the same preserved inputs should make differences explainable.

## Automation has boundaries

Deterministic methods are preferred where they are sufficient. Statistical or
language models may assist with narrow observations, but model output is not
authority. Identity resolution, hypotheses, and high-impact conclusions need
explicit evidence and appropriate human review.
